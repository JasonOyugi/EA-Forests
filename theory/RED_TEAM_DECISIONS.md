# Red-team decisions and unresolved questions

This note records the major conceptual questions raised during critique of the
mathematical paper and the decisions that should constrain future revisions.

## 1. Keep age explicit

**Question.** If diameter, height, and quality are already in state, is age
redundant?

**Decision.** Keep age. Use `da_t = dt` along a cohort. Age can affect growth
slowdown, height shutdown, mortality, management timing, and rotation decisions
in ways that are not safely reconstructed from size alone. Size and age can be
strongly correlated without being interchangeable.

**Boundary case.** A reduced model may omit age if the transition law is
estimated directly as a Markov law in size and quality and the omitted-age
assumption is empirically tested. That is a model reduction, not the default
state definition.

## 2. Clarify what the geographic space D means

**Question.** Is `D` East Africa, a single forest, or something else?

**Decision.** `D` is the compact geographic metric space on which a particular
model instance is defined. It can be a regional domain, country, estate, or
study landscape. A particular forest need not equal all of `D`; it can be the
support of the standing-tree measure inside `D`.

The metric is part of the modelling choice. Euclidean distance can be adequate
for biological proximity; road-network or travel distance can be more relevant
for delivered-cost calculations. These need not be the same metric in every
operator.

## 3. Distinguish finite particles from the limiting measure

**Question.** What does the superscript `N` mean?

**Decision.** In a finite interacting system, `X_t^{i,N}` is particle/tree `i`
in an `N`-particle approximation and

`mu_t^N = (1/N) sum_i delta_{X_t^{i,N}}`

is its empirical probability law when normalized. `N` is the discretization or
finite-population size used in the approximation. It need not equal the literal
number of trees in the entire market.

The limiting `mu_t` is unlabelled. Do not use the measure notation as though it
preserves individual identity.

## 4. Tree identity belongs in the observation/data layer

**Question.** Does the theory support identifying and following particular
trees?

**Decision.** Yes at the finite data/particle level, but identity is not a
primitive coordinate of the limiting measure unless a scientific decision
really depends on identity or lineage. Repeated observations can be associated
through an external tree registry, plot/tree key, or data-association model.

This distinction prevents the mathematical state from becoming an operational
database schema while still allowing repeated-tree calibration.

## 5. Competition should be law-dependent, not density-only by default

**Question.** Is it enough for competition to depend on population density?

**Decision.** Density-only competition is a legitimate special case, but too
coarse as a general biological statement. Use an interaction functional such as

`C(x, mu) = integral K_comp(x, x') mu(dx')`

or a lower-dimensional summary derived from the population law, for example
basal area, size-weighted crowding, stand-density index, or a local kernel.

Mapped repeated-tree data can support spatial pairwise kernels. If only stand
summaries are observed, a mean-field functional is the more identifiable model.

## 6. Separate process diffusion from measurement error

**Question.** Can Brownian diffusion simply stand for all observed spread in
DBH/height?

**Decision.** No. The diffusion term is a model for unresolved biological or
micro-site dynamics conditional on state and covariates. Observation error must
remain in the observation model. With only two time points it is generally not
possible to identify process diffusion cleanly from measurement error and
latent heterogeneity.

Start with an identifiable specification: constant or weakly state-dependent
noise in log-size coordinates, or a deterministic transition plus residual
variance. Richer diffusion should be earned by repeated measurements.

## 7. Common noise and idiosyncratic noise are different objects

Shared site-year weather or disturbance belongs in common noise `W^0`. Tree
specific heterogeneity belongs in idiosyncratic noise `B^i`. When simulating a
site class, the same common-noise realization must be shared across its trees.
Independently resampling the climate shock for every tree destroys the intended
correlation structure.

DBH and height shocks can also be correlated. A joint state model should not
implicitly assume independent diameter and height errors unless that is a
deliberate simplification.

## 8. Diffusion does not replace selection

Mortality and harvest alter the composition of the surviving population. Even
when state diffusion is present, the normalized population law contains
selection effects generated by state-dependent hazards. In simplified form,
for a test function `phi`, state-dependent removal contributes a covariance-like
selection term to the normalized composition.

The finite-mass measure is therefore not interchangeable with a probability law
for a representative survivor.

## 9. Keep finite-mass and probability geometries separate

Use Wasserstein geometry for normalized probability laws when total mass is
fixed. Use a mass-varying geometry such as Hellinger-Kantorovich for mortality,
harvest, recruitment, and unmatched supply where total mass can change.

Do not normalize away the very quantity a commercial decision depends on.

## 10. Do not overclaim global convexity

The inner log-allocation problem is convex under the stated assumptions.
Hard grades, species marks, harvest impulses, minimum lots, endogenous adoption,
and regime switching remain discrete or non-convex. The architecture should
exploit convexity locally rather than describe the entire problem as convex.

## 11. Keep the paper concise; move pedagogy here

The critique favoured a rigorous, math-first paper with a compact opening and a
clear operator chain. The repository companion is where slower derivations,
worked examples, numerical checks, and alternative special cases belong.

## 12. Open issues to preserve rather than hide

The current theory still leaves genuine research tasks:

- empirical identification of mature-tree growth and quality transitions;
- recovery/bucking kernels from tree state to log distributions;
- measurement-error-aware processor grade acceptance;
- local competition functionals supported by available spatial data;
- stable bargaining/outside-option models;
- posterior compression for tractable control;
- numerical error decomposition across particles, filtering, bucking, matching,
  and model discrepancy.

These are not defects to paper over. They are the empirical and computational
research programme implied by the theory.
