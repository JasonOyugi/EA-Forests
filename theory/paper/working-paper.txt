Abstract
We study a partially observed stochastic control problem for a spatial forestry system whose
economically relevant state is a time-varying distribution of tree sizes, qualities, locations, log
recoveries, processor specifications, and operating capacities. Standing-tree state is represented
by a finite positive measure on a marked tree space. Conditional McKean–Vlasov dynamics
describe size and quality evolution under common environmental noise and idiosyncratic variation,
while harvest and bucking induce a finite measure of heterogeneous logs. Processor demand is
represented by specification sets and grade-dependent price schedules; allocation of logs through
spatially heterogeneous operators is a capacitated partial-transport problem. The dual of this
matching problem yields marginal values of compatible supply, demand, and operating capacity.
Because both the physical state and structural parameters are imperfectly observed, the sufficient
decision state is a posterior law on a product of measure spaces. We formulate the resulting Bayesadaptive problem, characterize the topological and convexity structure of its components, state
the Wasserstein-space HJB equation for the conditional-law subproblem, and separate convex
inner allocation from non-convex learning and discrete regime decisions. A reduced-order East
African calibration is introduced only after the structural model is specified, and is interpreted
as prior-predictive evidence rather than as a primitive assumption. The framework therefore
treats biological growth, log recovery, processor compatibility, spatial transport, bargaining,
observation, and regime transition as distinct operators to be identified from data.

Keywords: stochastic control; McKean–Vlasov dynamics; Wasserstein geometry; unbalanced
optimal transport; Bayesian experimental design; distributionally robust optimization; forestry.
Suggested arXiv categories: math.OC, math.PR, stat.AP. Throughout, R+ := [0, ∞).

Introduction

A forestry market is a coupled biological and spatial allocation system. Standing trees evolve
stochastically; harvesting transforms trees into logs; logs are heterogeneous in diameter, length,
species, moisture, and quality; processors accept different subsets of that log space and attach
different prices to different grades. Transport and operating costs depend on location and contractor
productivity. The relevant market object is therefore a distribution of feasible material, not a scalar
inventory of hectares or cubic metres.
The mathematical problem has three nested layers. The first is a state-estimation problem:
infer the distribution of future tree and log attributes from incomplete inventory, remote sensing,
1

management records, and operational observations. The second is a matching problem: allocate
heterogeneous supply to processor-specific demand subject to spatial costs and quality constraints.
The third is a sequential control problem: choose sensing, matching exposure, contract variables, and
discrete regime transitions while the parameters governing growth, recovery, acceptance, response,
and operating cost are unknown.
The analysis is deliberately agnostic about a particular institutional or commercial implementation
until section 8. The objective is to identify the smallest mathematical structure that preserves the features which matter for optimization. The resulting model combines four familiar objects: controlled
McKean–Vlasov dynamics on Wasserstein space, finite-measure geometry for birth/death/harvest,
convex optimal transport for market matching, and Bayesian control on a belief space. Dynamic
programming for McKean–Vlasov control on probability-measure spaces is developed in Pham and
Wei (2016); Djete et al. (2019); unbalanced optimal transport between arbitrary finite positive
measures is developed in Chizat et al. (2015); Liero et al. (2015); and the control/learning coupling is
the classical dual-control phenomenon, reviewed recently by Meijer and Rantzer (2026) and studied
in active-learning form by Li et al. (2023).
The main contributions are analytical. First, we make the primitive state spaces and their
topology explicit, separating probability-law geometry from finite-mass geometry. Second, we
connect stochastic tree growth to processor economics through a harvest/bucking kernel and
specification-dependent log prices. Third, we formulate processor allocation as a convex unbalanced
transport problem and use its dual to define marginal supply value. Fourth, we formulate partial
observation over both physical state and structural parameters, rather than treating the physical
asset map as known. Fifth, we isolate which subproblems are convex and which are intrinsically
non-convex. Finally, we show how the existing hectare-level model is obtained as a coarse projection
of the richer state system and use East African data for a prior-predictive numerical calibration.

Two geometries are required and should not be conflated. The quadratic Wasserstein metric W2

For λ > 0, geodesic convexity gives uniqueness of a minimizer along the admissible geodesic

is also useful numerically because the convex transport subproblem can be solved separately on
each species/grade stratum and then coupled through processor and operator capacity constraints.

Identification and numerical approximation

7.1

What must be identified

The structural parameter vector contains at least four blocks:
θ = (θG , θB , θM , θC ),

(81)

where θG governs growth/quality dynamics, θB harvest/bucking and log-yield maps, θM processor

(82)

with a discrepancy process is standard in computer-model calibration (Kennedy and O’Hagan, 2001);
identifiability requires experiments that separate parameter effects from discrepancy (Wu et al.,
2018).
The data hierarchy is correspondingly explicit:
1. tree/plot data identify growth, size distribution, mortality, and quality transitions;
2. harvest/bucking records identify the map from standing trees to log distributions;
3. contractor records identify productivity and delivered-cost kernels;
4. processor intake records identify grade acceptance and realised price schedules;
5. repeated bids and alternative buyers identify bargaining/outside-option structure;
6. platform conversion and renewal identify willingness to pay and adoption elasticity.

7.2

Simulation architecture

A structurally faithful numerical experiment is a nested stochastic program over a measure-valued
state, not a single Monte Carlo draw over scalar parameters. For posterior particle s = 1, . . . , Nθ ,
one numerical path consists of:
1. draw θ(s) = (θG , θB , θM , θC ) from the current posterior and a common environmental path;
2. propagate a particle approximation of the conditional growth law (20), using the same commonnoise realization across trees in a site class and idiosyncratic noise at tree level;
3. estimate mortality/harvest events, then solve or sample the bucking map to obtain a random
marked log measure with dimensions and quality;
19

e ;
4. transform log volume to mass through (3) and construct Λ

and operator feasibility;

flows and dual potentials;
7. generate observations under the selected sensing/contracting action and update the joint state–
parameter posterior;
8. evaluate dynamic risk, continuation value and any scale impulse under the candidate policy.

by a learned conditional transition density. The empirical measure N −1 i δΞi approximates the
t
conditional law within a mark/site class; common-noise coupling must be preserved rather than
independently resampled across trees. Size-structured harvesting models provide a complementary
PDE/transport limit when individual diffusion is less useful (Wang and Yu, 2026), while empirical
diameter-distribution models can be used as static or initialization diagnostics (Teimouri et al.,
2019).
The allocation layer is conditionally convex. Its exact LP provides a benchmark; the entropytransport approximation enables Sinkhorn-type scaling (Chizat et al., 2016). Regularization bias
should be estimated by solving a subset of scenarios at decreasing ε and comparing both primal
value and dual potentials. Those duals act as a sensitivity oracle: if an outer sensing or coverage
decision changes (S, D, O) affinely, the dual variables provide supergradients of the concave matching
value and support Benders/cutting-plane decompositions. Discrete grade, minimum-lot, harvest
and regime choices remain outside this convex core.
The outer control problem can then be approximated by posterior scenario trees, sequential Monte
Carlo with rollout, stochastic model-predictive control, or fitted value functions on a low-dimensional
belief embedding. Reduced-order coordinates should be chosen from quantities with direct decision
meaning—for example local compatible supply Sj (R), matched-demand fractions χj , posterior
moments of growth/quality, and selected dual potentials—rather than from raw hectares. This is a
natural place to exploit state-space reduction without collapsing the tree/log specification structure.
For the current coarse benchmark, sample-average approximation is used only as a prior-predictive
diagnostic (Kleywegt et al., 2002). Correlated uncertainty is assessed with Shapley effects rather than
an independence-dependent Sobol decomposition (Iooss and Prieur, 2017). Numerical uncertainty
should be decomposed into at least five components: particle/discretization error in the biological law,
posterior/filter approximation error, bucking/taper-model error, transport regularization/scenario
error, and structural model discrepancy. Reporting a single Monte Carlo interval that mixes these
objects would obscure where additional data or computation is valuable.

East African numerical calibration

Only at this point do we impose current East African evidence. Internal analysis estimates
approximately 338,555 planted hectares in Uganda and 588,000 hectares in Tanzania, approximately
0.93 million hectares combined (Gatsby Africa, 2026a). A processor intelligence base identifies 37
20

Chinese-owned processors, approximately 5 million m3 /year of installed capacity, approximately
3 million m3 /year of current use, and typical utilisation around 50–60% (Gatsby Africa, 2026b).
These quantities initialize one numerical calibration; they are not assumptions of the general model.
Reported processor requirements occur in both cubic metres and tonnes; the structural model
therefore converts only through the state-dependent density/volume map in (3), never through a
universal tonnes-per-cubic-metre constant.
The available evidence already supports the need for the richer specification layer. Processor
records include heterogeneous raw-material descriptions, diameter/quality preferences, catchment
radii, current and required procurement volumes, utilisation, and sourcing constraints; the existing
roundwood model separately represents mensuration, felling, extraction, loading and haulage
primitives. In the internal processor-supply workbook, for example, some processors require
high-grade consistent roundwood while others explicitly accept smaller-dimension or lower-quality
fast-growing material, so a single regional price-per-tonne process is structurally misspecified (Gatsby
Africa, 2026d). The public repository also contains grade/specification data structures but labels
several records as dummy functional test data; those records are therefore excluded from empirical
calibration (EA Forests, 2026).
The same repository contains trial-site records indexed by age, genetic entry and site. They report
DBH, height, stem-form score, survival and volume-proxy summaries or distributions. These variables
can initialize a conditional growth/quality prior, but they do not identify a mature-tree transition
kernel or a processor-grade recovery kernel. Longitudinal remeasurement and harvest/bucking
observations are therefore required before the structural simulator can be treated as calibrated
rather than prior-predictive (EA Forests, 2026).

8.4

What is and is not identified

The current evidence supports the physical scale of the market, heterogeneous processor demand,
portfolio concentration, and several operating-cost primitives. It does not yet identify a region-wide
joint distribution of tree diameter, length-recoverable volume, quality, moisture/density, harvest
timing, and processor acceptance. Nor does it identify a stable bargaining function such as (47).
Therefore the tree/log/matching layer should be treated as a model to be estimated, not as a source
of invented precision.
This is also the strongest reason to work directly with contractors, operators, and processor
intake data. Those observations identify KB , c(λ, j; k), realised grade maps, density conversions,
acceptance rates, and the outside-option graph. Once these objects are estimated, the model can
value a mapped cluster of mature trees near a processor in the correct unit: expected matched
surplus and its dual shadow value, rather than hectares alone.

9

Conclusion

The natural state of a forestry market is measure-valued. Tree growth moves mass through sizequality space; harvest removes mass from the standing-tree measure and creates a log measure;
processor specifications define admissible regions of log space; spatial operating costs define the
edge weights of a supply-demand network; and market allocation is a partial transport problem on
those measures. Because the state itself is only partially observed, the sufficient decision variable is
a posterior distribution over both the physical state and unknown structural parameters.
This formulation separates several questions that are otherwise easily conflated. Biological
productivity is not commercial availability. Commercial availability is not processor compatibility.
Processor compatibility is not delivered surplus. Delivered surplus is not negotiated seller price.
24

Physical coverage is not information value. Each arrow between these objects is an operator that
can be estimated and stress-tested.
The present East African numbers are therefore best viewed as a reduced-order prior predictive
projection of the structural model. They motivate scale experiments but do not eliminate the
need to identify the underlying operators. The analytical advantage of the richer formulation is
precisely that new inventory, contractor, and processor data have a defined destination in the
model: they update growth laws, bucking kernels, matching costs, grade acceptance, dual supply
values, bargaining outside options, or service-cost functions. This makes the model progressively
less dependent on hectare-level proxies as the information system itself becomes more informative.

Reproducibility and data provenance

scale-reference, stress-test, and frontier outputs. The empirical asset distribution is calculated
from the Uganda CFR register. Aggregate processor and sector statistics are taken from internal
