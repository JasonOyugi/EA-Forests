# Chapter 8 summary - East African prior-predictive calibration

Only after the structural model is defined does the paper introduce its East
African numerical calibration. The calibration is a reduced-order,
prior-predictive experiment, not a claim that the full tree-to-log-to-matching
system has been empirically identified.

The working paper uses internal Uganda and Tanzania market evidence to motivate
the physical scale of planted forestry, processor capacity/utilisation, asset
concentration, and operating-cost primitives. It explicitly notes that current
evidence does **not** identify a region-wide joint distribution of tree size,
quality, moisture/density, harvest timing, log recovery, and processor
acceptance.

The reduced model reports distributions at 100k ha, 500k ha, and 1m ha. In the
paper's 50,000 prior-predictive draws, median recurring revenue is about USD 78k,
451k, and 850k per year respectively; median base OPEX is about USD 219k, 424k,
and 669k. Median joint operating surplus moves from negative at 100k ha to
positive at the two larger scales under the assumed priors. These numbers are
scenario outputs, not observed business performance.

A central calibration lesson is about cost identification. A naive pure power
fit to the three base-OPEX anchors suggests a strongly sublinear exponent near
0.47, but the same points can be fit by a large fixed component plus a nearly
linear variable component. Falling average cost therefore does not prove strongly
sublinear marginal coverage cost.

The paper also reports one-lever break-even thresholds and the revenue
multipliers needed to reach an 80% probability of nonnegative recurring
operating surplus. At 100k ha that robustness gap is much larger than at 500k ha
or 1m ha under the current assumptions.

The chapter's most important conclusion is negative but useful: the current
numbers motivate scale experiments, while the structural model tells us exactly
which missing observations are required before stronger claims can be made.
