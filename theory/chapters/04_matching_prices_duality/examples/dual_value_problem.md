# Toy problem - Why a tonne can have different marginal value

A processor needs at most 10 tonnes of Grade A and 100 tonnes of Grade B.
Suppose the net surplus is USD 50/t for A and USD 10/t for B.

Current compatible supply is:

- Grade A: 8 t;
- Grade B: 150 t.

Assume no other constraints.

## Question

What is the marginal value of discovering one additional tonne of each grade?

## Solution

Grade A demand is not saturated. One extra tonne can be allocated and adds
USD 50 of surplus. Its local supply shadow value is USD 50/t.

Grade B already has 50 tonnes more compatible supply than demand. One extra
tonne cannot increase the optimal allocation, so its local marginal value is
USD 0/t.

## Lesson

"One additional tonne" has no universal information value. Its value depends on
where it lies in the compatibility graph and which resource constraints are
binding.
