# Open problems and toy research questions

## Problem 1 - Stability of the tree-to-log map

Assume two standing-tree measures `mu` and `nu` are close in an appropriate
finite-measure metric and the harvest hazard and bucking kernel are Lipschitz in
state. Under what conditions can one bound the distance between the induced log
measures?

A useful result would look schematically like

`d_log(Lambda(mu), Lambda(nu)) <= C d_tree(mu,nu)`.

Why it matters: it would quantify how inventory-state error propagates into log
supply uncertainty.

## Problem 2 - Weighted propagation of chaos with mortality

Let particles carry unequal weights and experience state-dependent killing and
recruitment. Prove conditions under which the empirical finite measure
converges to the intended measure-valued limit.

Why it matters: operational inventories rarely sample every tree uniformly.

## Problem 3 - Value of measurement near grade thresholds

Take one processor threshold `d_s >= d_min` and a noisy diameter observation.
Derive how expected matching value changes as measurement variance decreases.
Study how the result depends on supply density near `d_min` and on the matching
dual value of that grade.

Why it matters: it links instrument accuracy directly to commercial value.

## Problem 4 - Endogenous bucking/matching fixed point

Suppose the optimal bucking rule uses downstream matching shadow values, while
matching depends on the log distribution generated by bucking. Under what
conditions does an iterative bucking-matching scheme converge?

Why it matters: recovery should respond to demand, but numerical coupling must
be stable.

## Problem 5 - Belief compression

Construct a small simulation in which the full posterior is available. Compare
policies based on:

1. full posterior particles;
2. posterior means/variances only;
3. matched-demand fractions plus selected dual values.

Measure the decision-value loss from compression.

Why it matters: the exact belief state is far too large for a production
controller.

## Problem 6 - Verification allocation

Given a fixed field budget, choose which forest blocks to verify. Compare:

- area-proportional sampling;
- uncertainty-proportional sampling;
- matching-dual-weighted uncertainty sampling;
- decision-focused EVSI.

Why it matters: this directly tests the paper's claim that field verification
should be selected by posterior uncertainty and decision value rather than by
universal census rules.
