# Chapter 6 summary - Reduced-order commercial projection

The structural model is too rich to calibrate fully with current data, so this
chapter defines a lower-dimensional projection for commercial analysis.

The reduced state tracks covered hectares, paying asset accounts, paying
processors, verification effort, compute/data workload, and matched processor
tonnes produced by the structural matching layer.

Asset-side value is summarized through managed spend, operational improvement,
and avoidable loss/risk. Current asset revenue is represented as a monetisation
rate applied to the managed-spend base of adopters. Adoption itself can be
price-sensitive; for a homogeneous logistic segment, revenue is strictly
log-concave in price and has a unique optimum expressible with the Lambert W
function.

Processor-side revenue is linked to matched supply, not all regional wood:
processor fee revenue is a fee per tonne of supply made commercially visible or
matchable. This gives a clean interpretation to today's scalar supply factors as
projections of the richer matching operator.

Cost combines common fixed cost, geography-specific fixed cost, variable
coverage cost, account costs, verification cost, and compute workload. A key
result is the fixed-cost dilution identity: falling average cost per hectare
does not by itself identify strongly sublinear marginal coverage cost.

Break-even is represented as a half-space in asset and supply monetisation rates
when the underlying bases are fixed. Risk is summarized through the revenue
multiplier required to reach a target probability of nonnegative operating
surplus.

The chapter is deliberately modest. These reduced variables are not declared to
be the fundamental state of the forestry market. They are operational
coordinates that can be replaced progressively as the structural tree/log/
matching operators become identified.
